\subsection{CESNET-QUIC22, session-disjoint}

Three tiers appear in Table~\ref{tab:main-cesnet_quic22} and the ordering is
the result.

\paragraph{Our model leads the deep tier and ties the classical tier.} FlowCon-X
reaches 0.781 $\pm$ 0.007 over eight seeds. It beats every deep baseline by
margins whose seed spreads do not overlap: $+0.021$ over FS-Net, $+0.076$ over
a bi-LSTM with attention, $+0.130$ over a Deep Packet-style CNN. It does not
beat gradient-boosted trees on the first twenty signed packet sizes, which
reach 0.790 in roughly three seconds of training against our thousand.
AppScanner, published in 2016, is 0.009 behind us at thirteen seconds.

\paragraph{Identifier probes dominate both tiers.} SNI alone reaches 0.967. We
repeat that this is the labelling ceiling rather than a competitor, but it
bounds what any result here means.

\subsection{5G Traffic: it is the approach, not the architecture}

The picture on 5G Traffic is different and more informative. Under
session-disjoint splitting:

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lr}
\toprule
Method & Macro-F1 \\
\midrule
Flow-statistics MLP (10 scalars, no sequence) & 0.568 \\
Deep Packet-style CNN & 0.575 \\
\textbf{FlowCon-X} & \textbf{0.547 $\pm$ 0.046} \\
FS-Net & 0.585 \\
Bi-LSTM + attention & 0.614 \\
AppScanner (2016) & 0.640 \\
Packet-size histogram (XGBoost) & 0.830 \\
\textbf{All flow scalars (XGBoost)} & \textbf{0.849} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\textbf{Every neural model lands between 0.55 and 0.62}, ours included, and a
plain bi-LSTM beats it. Gradient-boosted trees on thirteen flow scalars sit
0.23 above all of them. Four independent architectures --- convolutional,
recurrent, transformer-based and our dual-encoder --- fail by the same margin
on the same split with the same budget. This is not a defect of any one design.

The sharpest line is the MLP. It sees the ten flow scalars and no packet
sequence at all, scores 0.568, and matches the sequence models; XGBoost on
thirteen scalars of the same kind scores 0.849. Same information, same split, a
0.28 gap attributable purely to model class. Tree-based models outperforming
neural networks on tabular features is
established~\cite{grinsztajn2022trees}; here it is the entire story, on a
benchmark where it is rarely tested.

\paragraph{Why this corpus behaves that way.} The 5G service taxonomy groups
applications by purpose, and what separates those groups is aggregate volume,
which trees read directly. \texttt{metaverse} is Zepeto and Roblox;
\texttt{game\_streaming} is GeForce Now and KT GameBox. Packet-sequence
structure distinguishes applications, not purposes.

We tested that explanation. Changing only the label column --- identical
architecture, split, seed and budget --- moves our model from 0.547 $\pm$ 0.046
on the service taxonomy to \textbf{0.606 $\pm$ 0.054} identifying the
application, and moves the best classical baseline in the opposite direction:
XGBoost on flow scalars falls from 0.849 to 0.604, while XGBoost on twenty
packet sizes rises from 0.703 to 0.694 and becomes the leader. The two
families swap places exactly as the explanation predicts. Our loss narrows from
0.30 to 0.09 --- and does not close. Destination port alone scores 0.610 on
that task, above our model.
